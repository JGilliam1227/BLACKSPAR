The Line™

Here is what's actually happening.

The Constraint

This business is not constrained by missing systems. SOPs exist. A sales playbook exists. EOS is the operating framework. It is constrained by inconsistent follow-through on the systems that already exist — most visibly in the sales playbook and new-employee onboarding, which "aren't consistently followed." That single gap shows up everywhere else: deals stalling at contracting, founder-led sales as a 12-month-plus unsolved problem, and a sales team that missed its own target by a third.

01

The sales team's own target wasn't close. Goal was $6M; actual was $4M (Q18) — and the founder herself, unprompted, names the real reason as "lack of follow through" (Q62), not a missing system.

02

Deals stall at the same stage, named three separate times. Contracting is where prospects stall (Q12), what escalates to leadership most (Q30), and the costliest problem in the business (Q31) — the same bottleneck showing up in three unrelated answers.

03

The documentation exists — the enforcement doesn't. SOPs "work well" (Q50), but the sales playbook and new-employee onboarding specifically are named as inconsistently followed (Q51) — notable for a firm whose own business is teaching other companies to build and follow playbooks (Q8).

04

Two CRMs are both active. Salesforce and HubSpot (Q42), with a prior CRM effort already failed (Q45) — producing duplicate customer communication (Q47) and metrics that matter most (deals in pipe, Q39) also being the hardest to access (Q40).

05

Founder dependency reads lower on paper than it does everywhere else. Revenue concentration is reported at 25–50% (Q13), but top-of-funnel leads stop entirely if the founder is gone 30 days (Q34), and founder-led sales is the named unresolved problem (Q28).

Why This Matters Now

This isn't a business that needs new process built. It's a business that has already built the process and hasn't made it impossible to skip. That's a faster, cheaper fix than starting from zero — but only if follow-through is treated as the deliverable, not assumed as a byproduct of good intentions.

AI Opportunity Architecture™

Where AI creates leverage. What to do first.

Three implementations. Every one traces back to removing The Line — and none of them require building new process from scratch.

Opportunity 01 — Highest Priority · Directly removes The Line

Contracting-Stage Deal Rescue System

Current State

Deals stall at contracting — the stage most often escalated to leadership (Q30) and named as the costliest problem in the business (Q31).

Future State

AI monitors every deal that enters contracting, flags stalls against a defined SLA, and prompts the specific next action and owner — turning an invisible bottleneck into a tracked, time-boxed stage.

Estimated Value

$300K–$700K recoverable — a meaningful share of the $2M gap between the sales team's target and actual result (Q18) likely originates here.

Difficulty

Low-Medium. Mostly a workflow/alerting build on top of the CRM already in use.

First Move

Pull the last 12 months of deals that reached contracting and never closed; find the common stall point before building anything.

Impact: HighDifficulty: Low-MedTime to Value: 30–45 days

Opportunity 02

One CRM, Enforced

Current State

Salesforce and HubSpot are both in active use (Q42); a prior CRM implementation failed (Q45), producing duplicate customer communication (Q46, Q47).

Future State

One CRM is designated the system of record; the other is retired or demoted to a single, narrow use. Customer communication and deal data live in one place.

Estimated Value

$40K–$90K recoverable in reclaimed admin time, plus the pipeline visibility (Q39, Q40) leadership says it wants but doesn't have.

Difficulty

Low. Largely a decision and a migration, not new technology.

First Move

Decide which CRM wins based on where deal and contract data actually lives today, and set a 30-day retirement date for the other.

Impact: Medium-HighDifficulty: LowTime to Value: 30 days

Opportunity 03

AI-Assisted Playbook & Onboarding Enforcement

Current State

A sales playbook and new-employee onboarding process exist but aren't consistently followed (Q51) — in a business built on teaching other companies to follow theirs.

Future State

AI checks each new deal and each new hire's first 30 days against the existing playbook and onboarding steps, flagging what's been skipped in real time — instead of relying on memory or manager bandwidth to catch it.

Estimated Value

Directly targets the founder's own named root cause (Q62): lack of follow-through. Converts an enforcement problem into a visibility problem, which is far cheaper to fix.

Difficulty

Low. The playbook and onboarding steps already exist (Q50, Q51); this is a checklist and tracking layer, not new content.

First Move

Turn the existing sales playbook and onboarding doc into a step-by-step checklist inside the CRM, with owners and due dates.

Impact: HighDifficulty: LowTime to Value: 30 days

If nothing changes

The constraint keeps running the company.

The sales playbook and onboarding process keep existing on paper while deals keep stalling at contracting for reasons no one is tracking. Two CRMs keep drifting further out of sync, top-of-funnel keeps depending entirely on the founder, and next year's target gets missed the way this year's was — not for lack of a system, but for lack of following the one that's already been built.

If this is executed

Same business. Line removed.

Every deal that hits contracting gets flagged the moment it stalls, with a named next step and owner. One CRM holds the truth. The sales playbook and onboarding checklist are tracked, not assumed. Monday mornings start with forecasted deals closed instead of a guess — and for the first time, the gap between target and actual has a specific, visible cause instead of a general one.
